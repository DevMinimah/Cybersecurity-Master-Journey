## ⚠️ Disclaimer:
Every scenario is a simulation for practical educational purposes only. All activities are conducted in controlled learning environments to develop cybersecurity skills and knowledge. No real systems, networks, or data were accessed or modified.

##  Activity Type:
Scenario-based professional simulation: Analyzing organizational constraints to select appropriate Security Operations Center (SOC) models and assigning personnel to specific SOC roles based on skill profiles.

## 🎯 Lab Goal:
To evaluate and distinguish between standard SOC models (In-house, V-SOCaaS, Hybrid/Co-managed) based on real-world business scenarios, and to practice assigning security roles and tasks to ensure effective security operations.

## 🛠 Tools Used:
- SOC Model Evaluation Framework
- Role-Skill Matching Matrix

##  What I Did:
1. Analyzed SOC Models: Explored standard security operation models to understand their benefits, costs, and resource requirements.
2. Evaluated Scenario 1 (Marsh Entertainment): Assessed a growing entertainment company with distributed offices but severe constraints: limited budget, only two IT staff, no space for hardware, and inability to hire additional cybersecurity staff.
3. Evaluated Scenario 2 (Sunlight Enterprises): Assessed a large multinational with an existing on-premises SOC that is struggling to handle increased cyberattacks (ransomware) and the security demands of new cloud services.
4. Selected Appropriate SOC Models: Determined the most suitable SOC model for each scenario based on their specific operational needs and constraints.
5. Assigned SOC Roles: Reviewed applicant profiles and matched candidates to specific SOC roles and tasks to build an effective security team.

## 🔍 What I Found:
- Marsh Entertainment (Scenario 1): The combination of limited budget, lack of physical space for hardware, distributed offices, and minimal IT staff makes an MSSP (Managed Security Service Provider) or Virtual SOC model the optimal choice. This allows the company to outsource security monitoring and incident response without significant capital expenditure or the need to hire full-time specialized staff.
- Sunlight Enterprises (Scenario 2): This organization needs a Hybrid or Co-managed SOC model. Since they already have an on-premises team, they need a solution that augments their capabilities rather than replacing them. A co-managed approach allows them to offload cloud security monitoring and handle alert overflow to external experts, freeing up their internal team to focus on critical on-premises issues.
- SOC Role Alignment: Effective security operations depend on assigning the right people to the right tasks. Matching applicant skills (e.g., analytical thinking, technical expertise, communication) to roles like SOC Analyst, Incident Responder, or SOC Manager ensures efficient triage and response.

## 💡 What I Learned:
- SOC Model Selection: There is no one-size-fits-all SOC model. The choice depends heavily on organizational factors such as budget, existing infrastructure, staff expertise, and the complexity of the IT environment (e.g., cloud vs. on-prem).
- Scalability: As organizations grow or adopt cloud services, their security operations must scale. Hybrid models offer a practical path for maturing organizations to extend their capabilities without rebuilding from scratch.
- Resource Constraints: Limited resources (budget, staff, space) can be mitigated by leveraging external expertise through MSSPs, ensuring security coverage even for resource-constrained organizations.
- Role Specialization: A successful SOC requires a diverse team with specialized roles. Proper assignment ensures that incidents are escalated correctly and handled by personnel with the appropriate level of expertise.
