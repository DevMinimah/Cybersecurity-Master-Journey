# Module 01: Foundations of Cybersecurity and Computing

**Date Started:** June 20, 2026  
**Date Completed:** June 23, 2026  

---

## 🎯 What I Learned

### 1. Foundations & Core Frameworks
- Defined cybersecurity as an ever-evolving practice of protecting systems, networks, and data from digital attacks, requiring continuous learning to adapt to new threats.
- Understood its critical impact across three levels:
  - **Individuals:** Personal data protection
  - **Organizations:** Financial/reputational survival (global cyberattack costs projected at $10.5 trillion)
  - **Governments:** National security and cyber warfare
- Studied the foundational models of information security:
  - **CIA Triad:** Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability
  - **AAA Framework:** Authentication, Authorization, Accounting

---

### 2. Attack Vectors & Threat Categories
- Deconstructed attack vectors into three core components:
  1. **Vulnerability** (the flaw)
  2. **Mechanism of Action** (how it's exploited)
  3. **Pathway** (the delivery route)
- Categorized the four main cybersecurity threat domains:
  - Social Engineering
  - Unauthorized Access
  - System Design Failure
  - Malware
- Analyzed system design failures, emphasizing the dangers of:
  - Default credentials
  - Unpatched software
  - Cracked/pirated applications containing latent backdoors

---

### 3. Malware & System Infections
- Differentiated between malware types:
  - **Worms:** Self-replicating, no host needed
  - **Viruses:** Require user action and a host file
  - **Spyware:** Records activity
  - **Ransomware:** Encrypts data for extortion
  - **Logic Bombs:** Triggered by specific conditions/time
  - **Keyloggers:** Captures keystrokes
- Identified common signs of infection:
  - Performance degradation
  - Unexpected pop-ups
  - Unauthorized log-outs
  - Altered settings
  - Unexplained sent emails
- Established prevention protocols:
  - Keep antivirus enabled
  - Apply patches immediately
  - Strictly avoid cracked software

---

### 4. Physical Security & Social Engineering
- Explored physical access threats:
  - **Tailgating:** Following an authorized person without consent
  - **Piggybacking:** Following with their knowledge/consent
  - **Shoulder Surfing:** Observing data entry in public
  - **Dumpster Diving:** Searching trash for sensitive data
- Learned physical defenses:
  - Shredding sensitive documents
  - Destroying old ID cards
  - Maintaining strict badge-in policies

---

### 5. The Phishing Family & Red Flags
- Mapped the phishing variants:
  - **Phishing:** Generic email
  - **Vishing:** Voice calls
  - **Smishing:** SMS/text
  - **Spear-phishing:** Targeted individuals
  - **Whaling:** High-level executives
- Identified attacker red flags:
  - Artificial urgency
  - "Too good to be true" offers
  - Spoofed domains (e.g., G1FT instead of GIFT)
  - Generic greetings
  - Suspicious attachments/links
- Applied emotional trigger analysis: Attackers exploit **fear, curiosity, greed, and sympathy** to bypass logical thinking.

---

### 6. Risk Management & Treatment
- Defined risk management as the continuous process of identifying, assessing, and responding to risks to protect organizational assets.
- Mastered the four risk treatment strategies:
  - **Avoidance:** Eliminating the risk source
  - **Mitigation:** Reducing likelihood/impact via controls
  - **Transfer:** Shifting financial burden (e.g., cyber insurance)
  - **Acceptance:** Consciously living with the risk when treatment is impractical
- Calculated risk prioritization using the formula:
  - **Risk = Likelihood × Impact**
  - Determines whether a risk requires immediate remediation, planned fixes, or simple monitoring

---

### 7. Security Operations & Career Paths
- Explored operational controls:
  - Email filtering
  - SOC monitoring
  - Endpoint protection
  - Critical use of password managers over plain-text storage
- Mapped core cybersecurity roles:
  - **GRC Analyst:** Policies, standards, compliance
  - **Penetration Tester / Red Team:** Authorized simulated attacks to find vulnerabilities
  - **SOC Analyst / Blue Team:** Continuous monitoring, detection, and incident response

---

### 8. Professional Ethics & Legal Boundaries
- Understood the non-negotiable nature of confidentiality and integrity in handling sensitive organizational data.
- Learned the strict legal requirement of obtaining **explicit written permission** before conducting any penetration testing; unauthorized testing is illegal.
- Committed to minimizing operational disruption during security testing and adhering to professional industry codes of conduct (e.g., (ISC)², EC-Council).

---

### 9. Kali Linux Fundamentals & CLI Operations
- Explored Kali Linux as a specialized, open-source distribution designed specifically for security auditing and penetration testing, emphasizing the need for proficiency in both its GUI and Command Line Interface (CLI).
- Learned how to perform essential file and directory management using core commands for navigation (`cd`, `pwd`), creation (`mkdir`, `touch`), manipulation (`cp`, `mv`), deletion (`rm`), and content viewing (`cat`, `less`).
- Studied Linux file permissions and ownership, understanding the `rwx` (read=4, write=2, execute=1) permission model and learning how to apply changes using `chmod` and `chown` to enforce strict access controls.
- Understood user and group management workflows, including creating new groups (`groupadd`), safely appending users to groups without removing existing memberships (`usermod -aG`), and verifying access levels.
- Explored essential system and network utility commands for troubleshooting and monitoring, including connectivity checks (`ping`), network configuration (`ifconfig`), resource monitoring (`df -h`, `free -h`, `ps aux`), and package management (`apt update/upgrade/install`).

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways

1. **Cybersecurity is not a destination** but a continuous, evolving discipline. A professional who stops learning is quickly rendered obsolete by emerging threats.

2. **Human psychology is often the weakest link;** technical controls must be paired with robust security awareness training to defend against social engineering and phishing.

3. **Risk management is about making informed business decisions** (Avoid, Mitigate, Transfer, Accept) based on the mathematical reality of **Likelihood × Impact**, not about eliminating all risk entirely.

4. **Ethical boundaries and legal compliance are paramount.** A security professional's integrity and adherence to the law are just as critical as their technical skills.

5. **Understanding the distinct roles within a security team** (Red, Blue, GRC) highlights that cybersecurity is a collaborative, multi-disciplinary effort requiring diverse skill sets.
